The brief

What you know going in

The store's checkout service calls two others to do its job: an inventory check and a payment authorization. Checkout goes slow and fails in bursts, with no deploy behind it. Don't guess which service is at fault, let one trace show you.

You'll learn to
Tracing method
  • Confirm a slowdown in APM and localize it to one transaction.
  • Open a distributed trace and read its span waterfall.
  • Tell a service's own problem apart from a downstream dependency's.
  • Attribute the root cause to the span that actually carries the time and the error.

Step 5 · Name the root cause

            
The failure surfaces as a 502 from checkout, but in the trace the error is thrown in the payment span. What's the honest root cause?

            

              
              
              
            

            
Checkout returns the 502, but it only fails because the call it depends on fails. The error originates in the payment span; checkout propagates it. The root cause is downstream, which is why "fix checkout" would have gone nowhere.

          

          

            See it in New Relic
            
The errors live on payment

            
The errors are recorded on the /internal/payment transaction, not on checkout's own logic. Checkout's failures are downstream of them, in the same windows.

Step 6 · What next

            
You've proven payment is the culprit. What's the right follow-up?

            

              
              
              
            

            
Fix the payment dependency, and in the meantime protect checkout from it with a timeout, a retry or a circuit breaker, and a fallback message. Alert on payment's own health so the dependency pages someone before customers feel it.

          

          

            The takeaway
            
Blame the span that carries the failure, not the one that reports it

            
The service that returns the error is rarely the one that caused it. The trace is what lets you tell the difference.

          

        


      


      
      

        
Case closed

        
Checkout was the messenger. Payment was the cause.

        
Checkout returned 502s and ran slow because the payment service it calls was slow and failing. Nothing in checkout changed. You proved it by following one request's trace: slow checkout → its span waterfall → the payment span carrying the time and the error.

        
What you'd do next in the real world:

        
    
          
  • Investigate the payment service itself: its own traces, logs, resources and recent changes.
    • 
          
  • Make checkout resilient while payment is shaky: a timeout, a retry with backoff, a circuit breaker, and a clear message to the shopper instead of a hang.
    • 
          
  • Alert on payment's latency and error rate, so the dependency is caught at the source next time.
    • 
        

        
Why it worked: distributed tracing turns one request into a waterfall of spans, so you can point at the exact call that failed, across service boundaries, instead of blaming whoever reported the error.
